The first few seconds

What happens when someone opens your website

A lot happens before a visitor has decided anything. The order matters: under the DPDP Act, tracking needs a clear yes first.

The problem

Trackers fire before the click

  1. Page loadsA visitor arrives.
  2. Trackers fire analytics-scriptad-pixelsession-recorder Cookies are set. Nobody has said yes.
  3. Cookie banner appearsIt asks a question that has already been answered for them.
  4. Visitor clicks
  5. Too lateThe tracking already happened, whatever they choose.
The right way

Trackers wait for a yes

  1. Page loadsA visitor arrives. Nothing tracks them yet.
  2. Cookie banner appears“Accept” and “Reject”, equally easy to press.
  3. Visitor clicks “Accept”
  4. Trackers fire analytics-scriptad-pixelsession-recorder Only after a yes. If they click “Reject”, these never load.

The tracker names are generic examples. GazeVeil sees which of these happen by watching what your pages load and set before anyone clicks anything.

What we look for

The gaps a visitor's browser can already see

Each check is a written rule tied to the DPDP Act, 2023 or the DPDP Rules, 2025. If we can't tell for sure, we say so instead of guessing.

Tracking before consent

Analytics, advertising and session-recording tools that load, or set cookies, before a visitor has said yes.

No real way to say no

A cookie banner with an “Accept” button but no equally easy way to reject.

Pre-ticked boxes

Consent boxes on your forms that are already ticked before the visitor touches them.

The privacy notice

Whether you have one, whether visitors can find it, and whether it says what the Act asks, such as how to complain and how people can see, correct and delete their data.

How forms collect data

Which forms ask for personal details, whether they send them over a secure connection, and what consent wording and links sit beside them. We read forms. We never fill them in.

Basic security settings

A secure https connection, the standard security settings browsers use to protect visitors, and secret keys left in code that every visitor downloads.

Questions

Straight answers

Is this legal advice?

No. GazeVeil points out issues it can see on a public website. A report is a starting point for you, your developers and your lawyer, not a legal opinion.

Do you need access to my systems?

Not for the free report. It uses only what any visitor to your website can see. Deeper checks, in the detailed report, happen only with your signed, written permission and only within what it allows.

What is the DPDP Act?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 is India's law on how organisations handle people's personal data in digital form. It covers what you must tell people, when you need their consent, and how you must keep their data safe. The DPDP Rules, 2025 fill in the details.

When does it apply?

Penalties under the DPDP Act cannot start before 13 May 2027.

What do you do with personal data you see on my site?

We record only the type of data and how many we saw, never the data itself. It is thrown away the moment it is recognised.
